# Rule 4.5

> **Rule 4.5** **When applicable, use an article (the, a, an) or a demonstrative adjective (this, these) before a noun or a multi-word noun.**

Articles and demonstrative adjectives show the position of nouns and multi-word nouns in the sentence. Use articles and demonstrative adjectives correctly and do not omit them to make the text shorter.

**Examples:**

- [Non-STE] **Non-STE:** *Turn shaft assembly.*
- [STE] **STE:** Turn <u>the</u> shaft assembly.

- [Non-STE] **Non-STE:** *Data module tells you how to operate unit.*
- [STE] **STE:** <u>This</u> data module tells you how to operate the unit.

- [STE] **STE:** You can use equivalent alternatives for <u>these</u> items.

It is not always correct English to put an article before a noun. Do not use articles in general statements or concepts.

**Examples in STE:**

- [STE] **STE:** <u>Solvents</u> can cause damage to <u>paint</u>.

  (No articles before “solvents” or “paint.” The context does not give a specified solvent or paint.)

- [STE] **STE:** The machine operates with <u>high efficiency</u>.

  (No article before “high efficiency.” The sentence refers to a general concept.)

- [STE] **STE:** This software increases <u>performance</u>.

  (No article before “performance” because it refers to an abstract quality.)

In short sentences, it can be clearer to use articles before all nouns.

- [STE] **STE:** Install <u>the</u> nuts (2) and <u>the</u> bolts (3).

But sentences that contain a long series of items are clearer when you use the article only before the first noun in the series.

- [STE] **STE:** Discard <u>the</u> O-rings (3), gaskets (4), seals (7), and washers (9).

When you use the article in a series of items, always make sure that adjectives do not cause ambiguity.

- [STE] **STE:** Install <u>the new</u> O-rings (15), spacers (14), nut (13), and safety pin (12).

  (The article in this instruction tells you that all items in the series are new.)

<!-- page 85 | 1-4-9 -->

- [STE] **STE:** Install <u>the new</u> O-rings (15), the spacers (14), the nut (13), and the safety pin (12).

  (The articles in this instruction tell you that only the O-rings (15) are new.)

A definite article is incorrect before a noun when an alphanumeric identifier comes after it. This is because the alphanumeric identifier shows that it is a proper noun.

**Example:**

- [Non-STE] **Incorrect:** *Tag the circuit breaker 36L7*. <!-- sic: final period printed upright, not italic -->
- [STE] **CORRECT:** Tag circuit breaker 36L7.

> **Help:** If you are not sure about how to use the articles correctly in your writing, refer to a comprehensive grammar book, for example, “Practical English Usage” by Michael Swan. This type of resource gives clear explanations on grammar topics, which include how and when to use articles correctly in your text.
