# Part 1 – Writing rules

![A typewriter with a typed page titled "RULES FOR WRITERS"](../assets/part-1-cover-typewriter.jpg)

*Figure description: A photograph of a beige manual typewriter on a light surface in front of a light brown wall. A sheet of paper is in the typewriter, and a diagonal shadow falls across it. Text in the image (typed on the paper): "RULES FOR WRITERS / 1. Never use a metaphor, simile, or other figure of speech which you are used to seeing in print. / 2. Never use a long word where a short one will do. / 3. If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out. / 4. Never use the passive where you can use the active. / 5. Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word, or a jargon word if you can think of an everyday English equivalent. / 6. Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous. / George Orwell - Politics and the English Language" (the title of the essay is typed in italics).*

“If you simplify your English, you are freed from the worst follies of orthodoxy…<br>
And when you make a stupid remark, its stupidity will be obvious, even to yourself.”<br>
Orwell G. (1946). *Politics and the English Language.*

## Sections and rules

### [Section 1 – Words](section-1-words/README.md)

- [Rule 1.1](section-1-words/rule-1-01.md) — Use words that are: Approved in the dictionary; Technical nouns; Technical verbs.
- [Rule 1.2](section-1-words/rule-1-02.md) — Use approved words from the dictionary only as the specified part of speech.
- [Rule 1.3](section-1-words/rule-1-03.md) — Use approved words only with their approved meanings.
- [Rule 1.4](section-1-words/rule-1-04.md) — Use only the approved forms of verbs and adjectives.
- [Rule 1.5](section-1-words/rule-1-05.md) — You can use words that you can include in a technical noun category.
- [Rule 1.6](section-1-words/rule-1-06.md) — Use a word that is not approved in the dictionary, only when it is a technical noun or part of a technical noun.
- [Rule 1.7](section-1-words/rule-1-07.md) — Do not use words that are technical nouns as verbs.
- [Rule 1.8](section-1-words/rule-1-08.md) — Use technical nouns that are approved in your company, industry, or subject field.
- [Rule 1.9](section-1-words/rule-1-09.md) — When you must select a technical noun, use one which is short and easy to understand.
- [Rule 1.10](section-1-words/rule-1-10.md) — Do not use regional, slang, or jargon words as technical nouns.
- [Rule 1.11](section-1-words/rule-1-11.md) — Do not use different technical nouns for the same item.
- [Rule 1.12](section-1-words/rule-1-12.md) — You can use verbs that you can include in a technical verb category.
- [Rule 1.13](section-1-words/rule-1-13.md) — Do not use technical verbs as nouns.
- [Rule 1.14](section-1-words/rule-1-14.md) — Use American English spelling unless other official directives tell you differently.

### [Section 2 – Multi-word nouns](section-2-multi-word-nouns/README.md)

- [Rule 2.1](section-2-multi-word-nouns/rule-2-01.md) — Write multi-word nouns of no more than three words.
- [Rule 2.2](section-2-multi-word-nouns/rule-2-02.md) — When a technical noun has more than three words, write it in full. Then, you can use one of these methods to make the technical noun clear: Give a shorter form of the technical noun.; Use hyphens (-) between words that you use as one unit.

### [Section 3 - Verbs](section-3-verbs/README.md)

- [Rule 3.1](section-3-verbs/rule-3-01.md) — Use only the verb forms that are given in the dictionary.
- [Rule 3.2](section-3-verbs/rule-3-02.md) — Use only these verb forms and tenses of verbs: The infinitive form; The imperative form (command form); The simple present tense; The simple past tense; The simple future tense; The past participle form (as an adjective).
- [Rule 3.3](section-3-verbs/rule-3-03.md) — Use the past participle form as an adjective.
- [Rule 3.4](section-3-verbs/rule-3-04.md) — Do not use auxiliary verbs to make complex verb constructions.
- [Rule 3.5](section-3-verbs/rule-3-05.md) — Use the “-ing” form of a verb only as a technical noun or as a modifier in a technical noun.
- [Rule 3.6](section-3-verbs/rule-3-06.md) — Use the active voice. In descriptive writing, you can use the passive voice only if the agent is unknown.
- [Rule 3.7](section-3-verbs/rule-3-07.md) — Use an approved verb to describe an action, not a noun or other parts of speech.

### [Section 4 – Sentences](section-4-sentences/README.md)

- [Rule 4.1](section-4-sentences/rule-4-01.md) — Write short and clear sentences.
- [Rule 4.2](section-4-sentences/rule-4-02.md) — Do not omit words or use contractions to make your sentences shorter.
- [Rule 4.3](section-4-sentences/rule-4-03.md) — Use a vertical list for complex text.
- [Rule 4.4](section-4-sentences/rule-4-04.md) — Use connecting words and connecting phrases to connect sentences that contain related topics.
- [Rule 4.5](section-4-sentences/rule-4-05.md) — When applicable, use an article (the, a, an) or a demonstrative adjective (this, these) before a noun or a multi-word noun.

### [Section 5 - Procedural writing](section-5-procedural-writing/README.md)

- [Rule 5.1](section-5-procedural-writing/rule-5-01.md) — Write short sentences. Use a maximum of 20 words in each sentence.
- [Rule 5.2](section-5-procedural-writing/rule-5-02.md) — Write only one instruction in each sentence unless two or more actions occur at the same time.
- [Rule 5.3](section-5-procedural-writing/rule-5-03.md) — Write instructions in the imperative (command) form.
- [Rule 5.4](section-5-procedural-writing/rule-5-04.md) — When there is a condition that the reader must know about first, start the instruction with a descriptive statement. Then, divide that descriptive statement from the command with a comma.
- [Rule 5.5](section-5-procedural-writing/rule-5-05.md) — Write notes only to give information, not instructions.

### [Section 6 - Descriptive writing](section-6-descriptive-writing/README.md)

- [Rule 6.1](section-6-descriptive-writing/rule-6-01.md) — Give information gradually.
- [Rule 6.2](section-6-descriptive-writing/rule-6-02.md) — Use key words and key phrases to give your text a logical structure.
- [Rule 6.3](section-6-descriptive-writing/rule-6-03.md) — Write short sentences. Use a maximum of 25 words in each sentence.
- [Rule 6.4](section-6-descriptive-writing/rule-6-04.md) — Use paragraphs to show related information.
- [Rule 6.5](section-6-descriptive-writing/rule-6-05.md) — Make sure that each paragraph has only one topic.
- [Rule 6.6](section-6-descriptive-writing/rule-6-06.md) — Make sure that no paragraph has more than six sentences.

### [Section 7 - Safety instructions](section-7-safety-instructions/README.md)

- [Rule 7.1](section-7-safety-instructions/rule-7-01.md) — Use an applicable word (for example, “warning” or “caution”) to identify the level of risk.
- [Rule 7.2](section-7-safety-instructions/rule-7-02.md) — Start a safety instruction with a clear and accurate command or condition.
- [Rule 7.3](section-7-safety-instructions/rule-7-03.md) — Give an explanation to show the risk or possible result.

### [Section 8 - Punctuation and word count](section-8-punctuation-and-word-count/README.md)

- [Rule 8.1](section-8-punctuation-and-word-count/rule-8-01.md) — You can use all standard English punctuation marks but not the semicolon (;).
- [Rule 8.2](section-8-punctuation-and-word-count/rule-8-02.md) — Use hyphens (-) to connect words that are directly related.
- [Rule 8.3](section-8-punctuation-and-word-count/rule-8-03.md) — You can use parentheses: To make references to illustrations or text; To include letters or numbers that identify items on an illustration or in a text; To identify the work steps in a procedure; To include abbreviations; To give the singular and plural forms of a noun at the same time; To explain words or a part of a sentence; To include an alternative.
- [Rule 8.4](section-8-punctuation-and-word-count/rule-8-04.md) — In a vertical list, a colon (:) has the same effect on word count as a period and shows the end of a sentence.
- [Rule 8.5](section-8-punctuation-and-word-count/rule-8-05.md) — When you put text in parentheses, it counts as one word in that sentence.
- [Rule 8.6](section-8-punctuation-and-word-count/rule-8-06.md) — Count each of these elements as one word: Numbers; Numbers together with units of measurement; Abbreviations; Alphanumeric identifiers; Quoted text; Titles, headings, and text on placards and labels; Proper nouns of individuals, groups, organizations, and geopolitical entities.
- [Rule 8.7](section-8-punctuation-and-word-count/rule-8-07.md) — Hyphenated words count as one word.

### [Section 9 - Writing practices](section-9-writing-practices/README.md)

- [Rule 9.1](section-9-writing-practices/rule-9-01.md) — Use a different sentence construction to write a sentence when a word-for-word replacement is not sufficient.
- [Rule 9.2](section-9-writing-practices/rule-9-02.md) — Use each approved word correctly.
- [Rule 9.3](section-9-writing-practices/rule-9-03.md) — When you use two words together, do not make phrasal verbs.
- [Rule 9.4](section-9-writing-practices/rule-9-04.md) — When you select terminology or wording, always use a consistent style.
